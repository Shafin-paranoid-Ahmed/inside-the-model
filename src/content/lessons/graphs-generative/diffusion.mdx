<Step title="From static to data">
Every point in this cloud starts as pure random noise and is moved, a little at a time, onto the data.

{/* IDEA: the whole lesson in one loop: data dissolves into noise, then a denoiser walks noise back.
    WIDGET: DiffusionDenoiser mode="both" dataset="moons" denoiser="exact" showControls=[false].
    OVER t: forward half (points dissolve), hold on noise, reverse half (points gather onto the moons).
    LOOK: at the hold, there is no trace of the moons left, yet the reverse half rebuilds them.
    QUOTE: alpha-bar at tau = 1 (signal left) from the readout. */}
</Step>

<Step title="The forward process: adding noise">
The forward process destroys data on purpose: at each small step it shrinks every point a little and adds a little Gaussian noise.

{/* IDEA: x_t from x_(t-1) by scaling and adding noise with variance beta_t; after enough steps the data is
    a standard normal. No learning here.
    WIDGET: DiffusionDenoiser mode="forward" dataset="moons" showScore=[false].
    OVER t: tau runs 0 to 1, points blur; the side plot shows the data share sqrt(alpha-bar) falling and
    the noise share rising, with a cursor.
    LOOK: the side plot: the noise share passes the data share at one tau.
    QUOTE: the tau where the two shares cross (signal-to-noise 1) and the signal share at tau = 0.5.
    SHOW THE MATH: the one-step forward kernel q(x_t given x_(t-1)). */}
</Step>

<Step title="Jumping to any noise level at once">
You never have to run the noising steps one by one: a point at any noise level is the clean point, scaled down, plus scaled noise.

{/* IDEA: closed form x_t = sqrt(alpha-bar_t) x_0 + sqrt(1 - alpha-bar_t) eps, with eps from N(0, I).
    This is what makes training cheap: pick a random t, jump there.
    WIDGET: DiffusionDenoiser mode="closed" dataset="moons" focus=[index of one point, chosen in probe].
    OVER t: tau sweeps; for the focused point the two parts are drawn as arrows head to tail: the scaled
    clean point and the scaled noise vector eps (fixed for that point); the rest of the cloud is faint.
    LOOK: the readout with the formula filled in, and the noise arrow growing while the data arrow shrinks.
    QUOTE: x_0, eps and x_t for the focused point at tau = 0.5.
    SHOW THE MATH: derivation of alpha-bar as a product of (1 - beta) terms. */}
</Step>

<Step title="What a denoiser must know">
To undo the noise, a model needs one thing at every place and noise level: the direction back toward the data.

{/* IDEA: the best guess of the added noise (eps-hat) is equivalent to the score, the direction of higher
    data density. For the moons mixture it has a closed form, so the widget shows the ideal answer.
    WIDGET: DiffusionDenoiser mode="field" dataset="moons" denoiser="exact".
    OVER t: tau sweeps from 1 down to 0.02; the arrow field changes from "point to the centre" to "point
    to the nearest moon". Hover any spot to read the predicted noise.
    LOOK: at high noise all arrows point to the middle; near tau = 0.1 they point to the moons.
    QUOTE: the score and eps-hat at one hovered location at two tau values (fix the location in prose).
    SHOW THE MATH: eps-hat = -sqrt(1 - alpha-bar) times the score (Tweedie); link to the denoising
    autoencoder arrows in /learn/graphs-generative/autoencoders/. */}
</Step>

<Step title="Training a network to predict the noise">
A diffusion model is a network trained on one simple task: given a noisy point and its noise level, guess the noise that was added.

{/* IDEA: DDPM loss: pick x_0, t and eps, build x_t in closed form, regress eps-hat(x_t, t) onto eps with
    squared error. Simple regression, no adversary, no KL.
    WIDGET: DiffusionDenoiser mode="train" dataset="moons" denoiser="learned".
    OVER t: scrubs the worker's training snapshots: the learned arrow field at tau = 0.3 lines up with the
    exact field drawn faintly beneath; a log-scale loss curve has the matching cursor.
    LOOK: early snapshots point the wrong way near the moons; late ones match the exact arrows.
    QUOTE: loss at start and end, and the mean angle between learned and exact arrows at the end.
    SHOW THE MATH: L = E over t, x_0, eps of the squared norm of eps - eps-hat(x_t, t).
    SHOW THE CODE: an 8-line PyTorch training step. */}
</Step>

<Step title="Reverse sampling: denoising step by step">
To generate, start from pure noise and repeatedly remove a little of the predicted noise until clean points remain.

{/* IDEA: reverse process: each step uses the network's eps-hat to move x_t toward lower noise.
    Deterministic version (probability-flow ODE, DDIM-like) vs stochastic (ancestral, DDPM-like, adds
    fresh noise each step).
    WIDGET: DiffusionDenoiser mode="reverse" dataset="moons" denoiser="learned" sampler="ode" (sampler
    toggle visible).
    OVER t: 600 fresh noise points walk to the moons along their paths, with short trails.
    LOOK: switch to stochastic and compare the paths (jittery) and the end result.
    QUOTE: on-data fraction and moon shares (quality() in src/lib/gen/toy.ts) for learned ODE, learned SDE
    and exact ODE; these are the numbers the flow-matching lesson compares against. */}
</Step>

<Step title="From noise to an image and back">
The same process works on images: each pixel is one coordinate, and the slider moves the whole picture between static and a clean image.

{/* IDEA: an image is a point with many coordinates; noising and denoising act on all pixels at once.
    Uses the exact ideal denoiser for a small set of 8x8 glyph images (closed-form posterior mean), so
    it is honest that this denoiser can only reproduce images it was given (say so in one sentence:
    real models generalise because they are imperfect estimators trained on huge datasets).
    WIDGET: DiffusionDenoiser mode="image".
    OVER t: tau sweeps 0 to 1 and back; three panels: the noisy image x_t, the denoiser's guess of the
    clean image x_0-hat, and the added noise. Scrubbing the timeline is the slider IDEA.md asks for.
    LOOK: at mid noise, the guess panel already shows the glyph while x_t looks like static.
    QUOTE: the tau at which the guess first settles on the right glyph (from the probe). */}
</Step>

<Step title="Guidance: steering what gets generated">
Text-to-image models add a condition, such as a caption, and guidance turns up how strongly the samples follow it.

{/* IDEA: conditioning: the denoiser also receives a label c. Classifier-free guidance mixes conditional
    and unconditional predictions: eps-hat_w = (1 + w) eps-hat(x, c) - w eps-hat(x). Larger w: samples
    hug the condition more tightly, with less variety. In text-to-image models c is a text embedding;
    here it is one of two labels, "upper moon" or "lower moon".
    WIDGET: DiffusionDenoiser mode="guide" dataset="moons" guidance=[3] with selectors label (upper or
    lower moon) and w (0, 1, 3, 7). Exact conditional scores (mixture restricted to one moon).
    OVER t: guided reverse sampling from the same noise points.
    LOOK: raise w and watch samples crowd toward the middle of the chosen moon.
    QUOTE: share on the chosen moon and spread along it for w = 0 and w = 7.
    SHOW THE MATH: the guided score and why w = 0 is plain conditional sampling. */}
</Step>

<Step title="Common misconceptions">
A few ideas about diffusion models sound right but are not.

{/* NO WIDGET. Misconceptions:
    1. The model removes noise from one image in one shot. Actually it takes many small steps; one-shot
       guesses are blurry averages (step 7 guess panel).
    2. The forward process is learned. Actually it is fixed; only the denoiser is trained.
    3. The network predicts the clean image. Actually DDPM predicts the noise (equivalent
       parameterisations exist, but the loss in this lesson is noise prediction).
    4. Generation reverses the exact noise that was added. Actually the starting noise is fresh; the model
       produces new samples, not recovered training points.
    5. More guidance is always better. Actually high guidance reduces variety and can distort samples. */}
</Step>
