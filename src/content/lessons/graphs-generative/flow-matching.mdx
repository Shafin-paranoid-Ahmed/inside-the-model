A cleaner cousin of diffusion: learn the direction each point should move.

{/* SKELETON (Day 13 plan: plans/day-13.md). Widget: FlowField (to build). Same two-moons data
    (src/lib/gen/toy.ts). Linear path x_t = (1 - t) x_0 + t x_1 with x_0 noise and x_1 data (time runs
    noise to data, the opposite of the diffusion lesson's tau: say so once). Learned velocity MLP
    (input x, y and time features) trained in a worker; the exact marginal velocity of the moons
    mixture is closed form (src/lib/gen/toy.ts posteriorMean) and is used for the "marginal" step and
    as a check. Euler integration for sampling. Numbers from FlowField/sim.ts probes only. */}

<Step title="Noise that flows into data">
Flow matching generates data by letting points of random noise drift along a learned velocity field until they land on the data.

{/* IDEA: generation as transport: a velocity field v(x, t) moves every point; integrate from t = 0 (noise)
    to t = 1 (data).
    WIDGET: FlowField mode="flow" (learned field, 64 Euler steps).
    OVER t: 600 particles move with short trails; faint arrows show the field at the current time.
    LOOK: the paths are nearly straight lines, unlike the curved diffusion paths.
    QUOTE: on-data fraction and moon shares at the end (quality()). */}
</Step>

<Step title="Probability paths from noise to data">
Pair each noise point with a data point and slide it along the straight line between them: that defines a path for the whole cloud.

{/* IDEA: conditional path x_t = (1 - t) x_0 + t x_1; its velocity is constant, x_1 - x_0. At each t the
    cloud of all such points is a distribution p_t, blending noise into data.
    WIDGET: FlowField mode="pairs" (40 random noise-data pairs drawn as lines, plus the full cloud faint).
    OVER t: points slide along their lines from noise to data; loop.
    LOOK: lines cross each other, so two paths can pass through the same spot heading different ways.
    QUOTE: none beyond the path formula; name the pair count.
    SHOW THE MATH: the path and its time derivative. */}
</Step>

<Step title="One velocity per place and time">
A network can only give one velocity at each place and time, so where paths cross it learns their average direction.

{/* IDEA: the marginal velocity v*(x, t) = E[x_1 - x_0 given x_t = x]. Following it moves the cloud through
    exactly the same p_t as the crossing straight lines, but the individual paths no longer cross; they
    curve.
    WIDGET: FlowField mode="marginal" (exact field from the closed form, conditional lines faint behind).
    OVER t: path time t sweeps 0 to 1; the arrow field and a few particles following it.
    LOOK: at a crossing, compare the two conditional directions with the single arrow drawn there.
    QUOTE: at one fixed spot and t, the two conditional velocities and the marginal arrow (from probe).
    SHOW THE MATH: v* as a posterior mean; why matching p_t needs no pairing. */}
</Step>

<Step title="Regressing the velocity field">
Training is plain regression: show the network a point on a random path and ask it for that path's velocity.

{/* IDEA: conditional flow matching loss: squared error between v_theta(x_t, t) and x_1 - x_0. Its minimiser
    is the marginal field of the previous step, even though each target is one random pairing. The loss
    therefore never reaches zero.
    WIDGET: FlowField mode="train".
    OVER t: scrubs training snapshots; the learned arrows at t = 0.5 line up with the exact arrows drawn
    faintly; a log-scale loss curve with a dashed floor (the irreducible part, computed from the exact
    field) and matching cursor.
    LOOK: the loss levelling off at the floor, not at zero.
    QUOTE: start loss, end loss, the floor value.
    SHOW THE MATH: the CFM loss and the statement that its gradient equals the marginal loss gradient.
    SHOW THE CODE: an 8-line PyTorch training step (compare with the DDPM one). */}
</Step>

<Step title="Sampling by integrating an ODE">
To generate, start at noise and take many small steps along the learned arrows, a method called Euler integration.

{/* IDEA: sampling solves the ODE dx/dt = v_theta(x, t) with Euler steps x += h v. Deterministic: the same
    noise point always ends at the same data point.
    WIDGET: FlowField mode="sample" steps=[64].
    OVER t: particles take the 64 steps; each step's position is a dot along the trail.
    LOOK: the trail of one highlighted particle and its end point.
    QUOTE: on-data fraction with 64 steps. */}
</Step>

<Step title="How few steps can you take?">
Because the paths are almost straight, a handful of large steps already lands close to the data.

{/* IDEA: step count is the cost of sampling; straighter paths tolerate bigger steps.
    WIDGET: FlowField mode="sample" steps=[4] with a selector 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 64 and a small chart of
    on-data fraction vs steps.
    OVER t: particles jump in 4 visible segments; chart cursor on the current step count.
    LOOK: switch to 1 step and see where the samples land (the average, between the moons).
    QUOTE: on-data fraction for 1, 2, 4, 8 and 64 steps. */}
</Step>

<Step title="Comparison with diffusion">
Diffusion and flow matching move noise to the same data, but along differently shaped paths, which changes how many steps sampling needs.

{/* IDEA: Gaussian diffusion is flow matching with a different path (its schedule bends the paths); the
    straight path is simpler to integrate. Same noise, same data, two fields.
    WIDGET: FlowField mode="compare": left the diffusion probability-flow ODE paths (exact score, imported
    from DiffusionDenoiser/sim.ts, time-reversed to run noise to data), right the flow-matching paths,
    from the same 200 starting points, at an equal step budget selector 4 / 64.
    OVER t: both sets of particles move in sync.
    LOOK: the diffusion paths curve and overshoot at 4 steps; the flow paths stay near straight lines.
    QUOTE: average path length relative to the straight-line distance, and on-data fraction at 4 steps,
    for both. Probe first; if the gap is small, say so plainly.
    SHOW THE MATH: the diffusion path written as x_t = a(t) x_1 + b(t) x_0 alongside (1 - t), t. */}
</Step>

<Step title="Common misconceptions">
A few ideas about flow matching sound right but are not.

{/* NO WIDGET. Misconceptions:
    1. Flow matching paths are straight. Actually the per-pair paths are straight; the paths samples
       follow are curved where pairs cross (step 3).
    2. The loss should go to zero. Actually random pairing leaves an irreducible floor (step 4).
    3. Flow matching and diffusion are unrelated. Actually Gaussian diffusion is a special case with a
       different path.
    4. One sampling step is enough because paths are straight. Actually one step lands on the average,
       between the moons (step 6).
    5. Flow matching needs a noise-adding SDE. Actually it is defined directly by the path and is sampled
       with an ODE. */}
</Step>
