<Step title="Experts replace the dense MLP">

In a mixture-of-experts layer the single feed-forward network of a transformer block is replaced by several smaller networks, called experts, and each token uses only a few of them.

{/*
IDEA: the MoE layer is a drop-in replacement for the block's MLP (link /learn/transformers/transformer-block/). Show dense first, then the same plane with E experts.
WIDGET: MoERouter mode="dense". One wide MLP box; every token particle flows through it; readout "multiply-adds per token". A ghosted row of 8 expert boxes appears at the end of the cycle.
T: t loops tokens flowing from the plane through the dense MLP to the output; phase markers "Dense layer", "Same size, split into 8".
TRY: watch every token light up the whole dense box.
QUOTE: multiply-adds per token for the dense MLP and for one expert (from sim.ts constants).
MATH: y = FFN(x) vs y = sum over chosen i of g_i(x) E_i(x).
RESEARCH: R2 (one sentence of history, cited).
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="The router picks the top experts for each token">

A small router scores every expert for the current token, and only the highest-scoring few are run; their outputs are mixed using the router's weights.

{/*
IDEA: top-k routing. Router logits, softmax, keep top-k, renormalise, weighted sum.
WIDGET: MoERouter mode="route" k=2 (trained aux=0.01 run, final weights). Selected token's 8 router probabilities as bars; particles split to its 2 chosen experts with widths = gate weights; outputs merge.
T: t cycles through 12 sample tokens, one per beat; bars ease to the new token's scores.
TRY: pause on a token near a cluster boundary and see the two gate weights come out nearly equal.
QUOTE: for the first sample token: its top-2 experts, their softmax scores and renormalised gates (sum to 1).
MATH: g = softmax(W_r x); keep top-k; g_i / sum of kept g. CODE: 6-line PyTorch top-k router.
RESEARCH: R1, R2.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Router and experts learn together">

The router and the experts are trained at the same time, so each expert gradually specialises in the kind of token the router sends it.

{/*
IDEA: specialisation emerges from joint training; the plane is partitioned by "which expert wins".
WIDGET: MoERouter mode="train" k=2 aux=0.01. Plane background coloured by top-1 expert (--viz-seq ramp per expert index plus number labels at region centroids), loss curve below.
T: t = training step (snapshots every 20 steps); region boundaries move and settle; loss falls.
TRY: watch the regions line up with the six clusters.
QUOTE: loss at step 0 and final; number of experts that end up owning at least one cluster.
MATH: loss = task MSE + aux term (introduced in the next step; here aux shown only as a label).
RESEARCH: none beyond R1.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Without balancing, a few experts take everything">

Left alone, the router tends to send most tokens to a few experts, which then get more training and become even more attractive, while the rest sit idle.

</Step>

<Step title="A balancing loss spreads the load">

Adding a small penalty that grows when the load is uneven pushes the router to share tokens across experts, and newer models nudge a per-expert bias instead of adding a loss.

{/*
IDEA: Switch auxiliary loss; then aux-loss-free bias balancing.
WIDGET: MoERouter mode="balance" k=2 balance="aux" (a toggle in the figure switches balance="bias"; default aux). Same histogram as step 4, now with f_i and P_i drawn as paired bars.
T: t = training step; the histogram flattens towards the even line; the aux loss value counts down.
TRY: flip to the bias method and compare how even the load ends up and the final task loss.
QUOTE: max-expert share and final task loss for aux=0, aux=0.01, bias.
MATH: Switch loss alpha * E * sum f_i P_i, minimised at f_i = P_i = 1/E; bias update rule from R3.
RESEARCH: R1, R3.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="When an expert is full">

Real systems give each expert a fixed number of slots per batch, and tokens that arrive after the slots are full skip the expert entirely.

{/*
IDEA: capacity factor and dropped tokens.
WIDGET: MoERouter mode="capacity" capacity=1.0 (slider 0.5 to 2.0). Each expert as a row of slots filling with token particles; overflow tokens bounce to the residual path, drawn in --viz-neg.
T: t streams one batch of 240 tokens into the slots in arrival order.
TRY: drag capacity from 1.0 to 1.25 and watch the dropped count fall.
QUOTE: dropped tokens at capacity 1.0 and 1.25 for the aux=0.01 run, and for the aux=0 run at 1.0.
MATH: slots = capacity * tokens / E.
RESEARCH: R1 (definition), R6.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Active versus total parameters">

Because each token only runs a few experts, the model can hold many more parameters than it uses for any one token.

{/*
IDEA: capacity grows with E, compute per token grows with k.
WIDGET: MoERouter mode="params" k=2 (sliders E 1 to 64, k 1 to 4). Two bars: total parameters and active parameters per token, plus memory needed vs multiply-adds per token.
T: t sweeps E from 1 to 64 at fixed k; the total bar grows, the active bar stays flat.
TRY: set k to 1 and E to 64 and read the ratio of total to active.
QUOTE: computed totals for E = 8, k = 2 and E = 64, k = 1 for the toy layer sizes.
MATH: total = shared + E * P_expert; active = shared + k * P_expert.
RESEARCH: R5 (one or two real model-card examples, dated and cited; none written from memory), R6 (memory still holds all experts).
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Common misconceptions">

Some common beliefs about mixtures of experts are wrong.

{/*
NO WIDGET. Misconceptions:
1. Each expert is a human-readable specialist (e.g. "the maths expert"). Actually specialisation is often by token type; cite what R4/R5 sources report.
2. MoE models are cheap to serve. Compute per token is low, but all experts must be in memory (step 7, R6).
3. Routing happens once per sequence. It happens per token, per MoE layer.
4. More experts always helps. Balancing, capacity and communication costs grow (steps 4 to 6).
*/}

</Step>
