- Compute-optimal allocation: spend more samples on harder problems (Snell et al. 2024).
- RL with verifiable rewards: REINFORCE with reward 1 for a checked-correct answer.

<Step title="Writing out the steps is extra computation">

A network does the same amount of work for every token it writes, so writing intermediate steps gives it more steps of computation before the final answer.

{/*
IDEA: chain of thought as extra serial compute. A model that must answer in one shot has a fixed computation budget; a model that writes running totals gets n small steps. Link /learn/neural-networks/mlp/ for the MLP.
WIDGET: TestTimeCompute mode="cot" (no other props). Left: the digit list and the STEP model writing its running totals token by token; right: accuracy against list length n for DIRECT vs STEP (greedy decoding, T=0).
T: t sweeps n from 2 to 12; the chain for the current n writes itself out, one running total per beat, and the accuracy curves draw up to the current n.
TRY: watch the gap open as n grows: the DIRECT curve falls while the STEP curve stays high.
QUOTE (from sim.ts, never guessed): DIRECT and STEP accuracy at n = 2, 6 and 12 on the fixed test set; tokens written per answer (1 vs n).
MATH: none beyond "work per token is constant". CODE: tiny PyTorch loop applying the step model n times.

*/}

<Step title="Sample many answers and take a vote">

If a model sometimes slips on a step, asking it several times and keeping the most common final answer often fixes the slip.

{/*
IDEA: self-consistency. Sampling the STEP model at temperature T gives chains that sometimes go wrong; independent mistakes rarely agree, so the majority answer is right more often than one sample.
WIDGET: TestTimeCompute mode="vote" n=8 temperature=1. Rows of sampled chains (wrong steps outlined with --viz-neg), final answers dropping into a 10-bin histogram, majority bin highlighted with --accent.
T: t = number of samples N, played on a log scale from 1 to 64; each new chain appears as a row, histogram bars grow, the accuracy-vs-N curve (log x axis) draws.
TRY: pause at N = 1 and N = 15 and compare the majority accuracy.
QUOTE: single-sample accuracy, majority-vote accuracy at N = 5, 15, 64 for n = 8, T = 1.
MATH: majority vote = argmax over answers of the count; "Show the math" with the binomial argument for two answers.
RESEARCH: R3; cite Wang et al. arXiv 2203.11171 (confirmed 2026-10-03) for self-consistency.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="A checker beats a vote">

When answers can be checked, keeping any sample that passes the check improves faster with more samples than voting does, and a careless checker gives some of that back.

{/*
IDEA: best-of-N with a verifier. Exact verifier: coverage (pass@N) keeps rising. Majority vote plateaus. A noisy verifier (accepts a wrong answer with probability f, rejects a right one with probability r) plateaus lower.
WIDGET: TestTimeCompute mode="verify" n=10 temperature=1.2 verifierNoise=0.1. Three curves vs N (log x): exact checker, noisy checker, majority vote.
T: t = N from 1 to 256 (log); a cursor moves along the curves; sampled chains flash green tick / orange cross as the checker marks them.
TRY: find the N where the majority-vote curve stops improving.
QUOTE: the three accuracies at N = 1, 16, 256; the closed-form pass@N 1 - (1 - p)^N at the measured p, which the exact-checker curve must match.
MATH: pass@N derivation for independent samples.
RESEARCH: R3, R4. Brown et al. 2407.21787 report majority vote and reward models plateauing; quote only what the paper says.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Spend more thinking on harder problems">

With a fixed budget of samples, giving more of them to the hard problems and fewer to the easy ones solves more problems overall.

{/*
IDEA: compute-optimal test-time scaling. Easy problems are solved with one sample; hard ones need many. Uniform allocation wastes samples on easy problems.
WIDGET: TestTimeCompute mode="budget" budget=320. A set of 40 problems with lengths n from 2 to 12; two allocations side by side: uniform (8 each) vs adaptive (estimated from a few probe samples, then reallocated). Bars per problem show samples given, coloured by solved/unsolved.
T: t plays the budget being spent sample by sample under both policies; solved counts tick up.
TRY: compare how many problems each policy has solved when the budget runs out.
QUOTE: problems solved under uniform vs adaptive at the full budget, and at half the budget.
MATH: none; CODE: the allocation rule (probe, estimate difficulty, reallocate).
RESEARCH: R3, Snell et al. 2408.03314 (their "compute-optimal" strategy and its conditions; no numbers from memory).
*/}

<Step title="Training a model to reason with checkable rewards">

Reasoning models are trained with reinforcement learning on problems whose answers can be checked automatically, so the reward is simply whether the final answer was right.

{/*

<Step title="Common misconceptions">

A few ideas about reasoning models sound right but are not.

{/*
NO WIDGET. Intended misconceptions (write as "You might think X. Actually Y."):
1. The written chain of thought is exactly how the model got its answer. (Faithfulness is an open question: R5, Baker et al. 2503.11926.)
2. More samples always help. (Majority vote and noisy verifiers plateau: step 3 numbers.)
3. Reasoning models are a new architecture. (Same transformer; the difference is training and inference-time budget: R1.)
4. Test-time compute replaces a bigger model in all cases. (Snell et al. find it depends on problem difficulty: R3.)
*/}
