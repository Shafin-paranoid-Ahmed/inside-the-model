Give the model an open book instead of asking it to recall from memory.

<Step title="Why a model needs an open book">

A language model can only repeat what was in its training data, so questions about private or recent documents need those documents supplied at question time.

<Step title="The pipeline: embed, search, retrieve, generate">

RAG embeds the question, finds the closest passages, pastes them into the prompt and lets the model answer from that text.

{/*
IDEA: the four stages, end to end, on the same question. Embeddings and search are the real LSA + cosine from the vector-search lesson (src/lib/applied/embed.ts).
WIDGET: RAGPipeline mode="pipeline" query="How much is the late fee for the pressure washer?" k=3.
GENERATOR (honesty): an extractive reader, labelled in the figure "Reader: copies the best-supported sentence from the passages (not a language model)". It scores every sentence of the assembled context against the question (cosine + keyword overlap), outputs the best one verbatim with its passage id as a citation, and outputs "Not in the passages" below a threshold. Deterministic.
MOTION over t: particle flows along the pipeline: question -> 16-number vector -> scan lights up the 3 nearest passages with their cosines -> passages slide into a prompt template -> the reader highlights its chosen sentence and the answer appears with [doc id].
TRY: edit the question; the whole pipeline replays.
QUOTE: the three cosines, the chosen sentence's score, the cited passage id.
SHOW THE CODE: 12-line Python RAG loop (embed, top-k, format prompt, call model) with the model call left as a function stub, matching the widget's stages.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Chunking: how big is a passage?">

Documents are split into chunks before embedding, and the chunk size decides whether the answer and its context stay together.

{/*
IDEA: small chunks = precise match but the answer may be cut from the sentence that qualifies it; big chunks = context kept but the embedding averages several topics and matches worse. Overlap helps at borders.
WIDGET: RAGPipeline mode="chunking" query="Can I take the pressure washer for a whole week?" chunk="sweep". Shows one long handbook page split by a moving chunk size (fixed-size word windows with overlap 0 or 20%).
MOTION over t: chunk size sweeps 20 -> 40 -> 80 -> 160 words; chunk borders slide over the page; the rank of the chunk containing the answer and its cosine update at each size; a small chart plots answer-chunk rank vs chunk size.
TRY: toggle overlap on and see the border case fixed.
QUOTE: answer-chunk rank and cosine at each size; the size where the answer and its exception ("except on public holidays") first land in separate chunks.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Putting retrieved text in the prompt">

The retrieved passages are pasted into a prompt template with the question, and that prompt has to fit in a fixed context window.

{/*
IDEA: prompt assembly: system text + passages (with ids, delimited) + question + instruction to cite and to say "not found". Every piece costs tokens; the context window is a hard budget.
WIDGET: RAGPipeline mode="prompt" k=5 budget=256. Shows the assembled prompt as text with each passage in a delimited block, and a stacked bar of token counts (instructions, each passage, question) against the budget line. Token counting: the Day 9 BPE tokenizer if present, else a word-piece count labelled "words" (decide in the plan, never call words tokens).
MOTION over t: passages are appended one by one in rank order, the stacked bar grows, passage 5 crosses the budget line and is cut (shown truncated).
TRY: raise k from 3 to 8 and see which passages no longer fit.
QUOTE: tokens per passage, total, and which passage was cut at k = 5.

</Step>

<Step title="When retrieval fails, the answer fails">

If the right passage is not retrieved, the generator cannot use it, however good it is.

{/*
IDEA: failure mode 1, retrieval miss: a question phrased with words the embedding maps poorly (or an exact item code), the answer passage ranks 7th, k = 3 misses it, the reader answers from the wrong passage or says not found. Hybrid search (BM25 + embedding, reciprocal rank fusion) fixes this one.
WIDGET: RAGPipeline mode="miss" query="What is the deposit for item PW-200?" k=3 hybrid=false, with a hybrid toggle.
MOTION over t: as in pipeline, but the correct passage (outlined --accent, known from the test set) is visibly below the k cut line; then with hybrid on, the fused ranking animates and it moves up into the top 3.
TRY: switch hybrid on.
QUOTE: answer passage rank under embedding, BM25 and fused ranking; reader output before and after.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="When the context is the problem">

Retrieving the right passage is not enough if it is cut off by the budget or contradicted by other retrieved text.

{/*
IDEA: failure mode 2, context limits: with a small budget the right passage is truncated; with stale and current versions of a rule both retrieved, the reader picks the higher-scoring stale one. Real long-context models also use information in the middle of a long context less reliably (Liu et al. 2023, "Lost in the Middle", VERIFY arXiv:2307.03172): cite it, do NOT simulate it, the extractive reader has no position bias.
WIDGET: RAGPipeline mode="conflict" query="What are the opening hours on Saturday?" k=4 budget=160. Corpus contains an old and a new hours notice (dated).
MOTION over t: retrieval brings both notices; budget cuts the newer one partially; reader cites the old one; then a "prefer newest date" re-rank (metadata filter) is applied and the answer changes.
TRY: raise the budget to 320.
QUOTE: both notices' cosines and token counts, the answer before and after the date filter.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Evaluating a RAG system">

A RAG system is evaluated in two parts: did retrieval find the evidence, and is the answer correct and supported by it.

<Step title="Common misconceptions">

A few beliefs about RAG that do not hold up.

{/*
NO WIDGET. Intended misconceptions:
1. "RAG stops hallucination." It reduces unsupported answers when retrieval works; the model can still ignore or misread the passages, and a miss gives it nothing.
2. "Bigger k is always better." More passages cost tokens, can push out the right one and add distractors (measured in the eval step).
3. "RAG teaches the model new facts." Weights do not change; remove the passage and the knowledge is gone (contrast with /learn/applied/lora-qlora/).
4. "If retrieval found it, the answer is right." Reader and retriever fail separately; evaluate both.
5. "Chunk size does not matter much." It decides what the embedding averages (chunking step).
*/}

</Step>
