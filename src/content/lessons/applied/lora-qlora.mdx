Fine-tune a huge model by training only a sliver of extra parameters.

<Step title="What full fine-tuning costs">

Full fine-tuning updates every weight, so it needs memory for the weights, their gradients and the optimizer's state for each one.

<Step title="The low-rank update: W + BA">

LoRA freezes the original weight matrix and learns its change as the product of two thin matrices, B and A, whose inner size r is small.

{/*
IDEA: W' = W + (alpha / r) B A, with B (d x r), A (r x k), B initialised to 0 so training starts exactly at the base model. Rank r limits the update to r directions.
WIDGET: LoRALab mode="lowrank" r=2. Real matrices: base W (32x32 heat map), B (32x2), A (2x32), product BA (32x32) shown as the outer-product sum of r rank-1 layers, W + BA.
MOTION over t: the r rank-1 layers (column of B times row of A) are drawn one by one and stacked into BA; then BA adds onto W.
TRY: change r between 1, 2, 4 and 8 and see the update's structure (stripes for r = 1).
QUOTE: parameters in BA (2 x 32 x r) vs in W (1024); rank of BA (r).
SHOW THE MATH: h = W x + (alpha/r) B A x; parameter count r(d + k) vs dk.
SHOW THE CODE: a 10-line PyTorch LoRALinear (frozen weight, A random, B zeros), matching the sim.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Why low rank is enough">

The change that full fine-tuning makes is often close to low rank, which is why a rank-r update can capture most of it.

{/*
IDEA: SVD of the full fine-tuning delta (W_full − W0): singular values fall off fast when the task change is low-rank; the energy captured by the top r singular values. Then the same with a task whose true change is full rank, where small r fails (honest counter-case).
WIDGET: LoRALab mode="spectrum" task="low-rank" with a task toggle ("low-rank change" / "full-rank change"). Bar chart of the 32 singular values (log axis), cumulative energy line, and the final LoRA loss at each r.
MOTION over t: singular values rise in order; the cumulative energy line draws; a marker steps through r = 1, 2, 4, 8 with the matching LoRA loss.
TRY: switch to the full-rank task.
QUOTE: energy in the top 2 singular values for each task; LoRA r = 2 loss for each task.
SOURCE: Hu et al. 2021 (LoRA) report that very low ranks suffice on their benchmarks, arXiv:2106.09685; Aghajanyan et al. 2020, "Intrinsic Dimensionality Explains the Effectiveness of Language Model Fine-Tuning", arXiv:2012.13255.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Merging: no extra cost at inference">

After training, the product BA can be added into W once, so the fine-tuned model runs exactly as fast as the original.

{/*
IDEA: merge W' = W + (alpha/r) BA; outputs identical to the unmerged adapter up to float rounding; adapters can be swapped by subtracting one and adding another.
WIDGET: LoRALab mode="merge" r=4. Input x passes through two paths (W x + B A x, and W' x); output vectors side by side; max absolute difference readout.
MOTION over t: particles flow along both paths; outputs fill in; then the merge animation folds BA into W and the two-path diagram becomes one path.
TRY: swap to a second adapter (task C) and back.
QUOTE: max absolute difference between merged and unmerged outputs (rounding level); multiply-adds per input for each path.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="QLoRA: a 4-bit base model">

QLoRA stores the frozen base weights in 4 bits to cut memory further, and trains LoRA adapters on top in higher precision.

{/*

Double quantization of the scales mentioned in prose only.
WIDGET: LoRALab mode="quantize" scheme="nf4". Histogram of W0's weights with the 16 level positions as vertical lines; each weight's rounding error; MSE readout; toggle int4 vs nf4.
MOTION over t: one block is highlighted, normalised by its absmax, weights snap to their nearest level (dots slide), error bars appear; then all blocks at once and the MSE settles.

<Step title="Adapters recover what quantization loses">

Training LoRA on top of the quantized base lets the adapter absorb part of the quantization error along with the new task.

<Step title="Fine-tune, prompt or retrieve?">

Fine-tuning changes how a model behaves and needs training examples; prompting and retrieval change what it sees and need none.

<Step title="Common misconceptions">

A few beliefs about efficient fine-tuning that do not hold up.

{/*
NO WIDGET. Intended misconceptions:
1. "LoRA makes the model smaller." The base model is unchanged in size; only the trainable part is small.
2. "LoRA is slower at inference." Merged, it costs nothing extra.
3. "4-bit means a quarter of the quality." Measured weight error is small with NF4 and the adapter compensates part of it (quote sim).
4. "Fine-tuning is the way to add knowledge." Facts that change belong in retrieval; fine-tuning mainly shapes behaviour (cite a source or phrase as guidance).
5. "Rank should be as high as possible." Past the task's intrinsic rank, extra rank adds parameters without lowering loss (spectrum step).
*/}

</Step>
