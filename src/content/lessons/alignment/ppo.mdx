How can an agent reuse a good batch without chasing it too far?

<Step title="An advantage compares an action with the usual outcome">

An advantage estimates how much better an action turned out than the critic expected from that state, so its sign tells the policy whether to favour or discourage that action.

{/*
IDEA: A(s,a) = Q(s,a) - V(s); connect the actor-critic TD error to generalized advantage estimation (GAE), a discounted sum of later TD errors with lambda setting its horizon.
WIDGET: GridWorldLab mode="ppo-advantage" gamma=0.9 lambda=0.95 batch=64 seed=29. One batch trajectory beside rewards, frozen critic values, TD errors and selected-step advantage contributions.
MOTION over t: walk forward, reveal each TD error, accumulate GAE backward with decaying contribution widths, then reveal the selected action's signed advantage.
TRY: compare the selected action's advantage at lambda 0 and 0.95.
NUMBERS: selected r, V(s), V(s'), delta and A at both lambdas; probe a batch containing positive and negative advantages. Correctly mask termination and bootstrap at time-limit truncations.
SHOW THE MATH: delta_t = r + gamma V(s') - V(s); A-hat_t = sum_l (gamma lambda)^l delta_(t+l) with episode masks. SHOW THE CODE: backward GAE recurrence.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Keep the policy that collected the batch">

PPO saves the old action probabilities when it collects a batch, then compares each new probability with that saved value using a ratio.

{/*
IDEA: rho_t(theta) = pi_theta(a_t|s_t) / pi_old(a_t|s_t); rho starts at 1, old log-probabilities and advantages stay fixed during optimization; denominator is the collection policy, not a permanent reference model.
WIDGET: GridWorldLab mode="ppo-ratio" batch=64 epochs=4 focus="1,1" seed=29. Old probability bars as labelled outlines; new bars filled; selected action shows old/new log-probability and ratio.
MOTION over t: collect actions, freeze the old bars, then reveal successive new-policy states; the ratio marker moves only with the new probability.
TRY: select another sampled action and check its numerator and denominator.
NUMBERS: old and new probabilities, their ratio, and ratio 1 at the start; compute all readouts from the displayed policy.
SHOW THE MATH: rho = exp(log pi_new - log pi_old). SHOW THE CODE: storing detached old log-probabilities at collection.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Clip the incentive to increase a good action">

For a positive advantage, PPO stops adding reward to its objective once the action's probability ratio grows beyond the upper clip threshold.

{/*
IDEA: min(rho A, clip(rho, 1-epsilon, 1+epsilon) A); for A positive the favourable direction plateaus above 1+epsilon, while decreases still hurt the objective.
WIDGET: PPOLab mode="clip-positive" advantage=1 epsilon=0.2 ratios="0.5,1.6". Two surrogate curves, unclipped and clipped, with a moving ratio cursor and the active branch labelled; use --viz-pos for the advantage sign.
MOTION over t: ratio sweeps across 0.5 to 1.6; both curves trace, then the clipped objective's cursor enters its upper plateau while the unclipped line continues rising.
TRY: compare the objective at ratios 1.2 and 1.5.
NUMBERS: both objective values and the clipped derivative at those ratios; probe exact branch endpoints, not invented training statistics.
SHOW THE MATH: the clipped surrogate and its positive-advantage piecewise form.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Negative advantages clip in the other direction">

For a negative advantage, PPO stops rewarding further decreases in the action's probability once its ratio falls below the lower clip threshold.

{/*
IDEA: with A negative, the minimum chooses a plateau below 1-epsilon; increasing a bad action's probability still worsens the objective, even beyond the upper band.
WIDGET: PPOLab mode="clip-negative" advantage=-1 epsilon=0.2 ratios="0.5,1.6". Same axes as the preceding step, negative-advantage curves and explicit lower plateau; distinct signed default and mode.
MOTION over t: cursor moves from ratio 1 toward 0.5, then toward 1.6, revealing the favourable decrease plateau and the penalized increase segment.
TRY: compare ratios 0.6 and 1.4 and identify where the gradient is still active.
NUMBERS: objective and derivative at ratios 0.6, 0.8, 1 and 1.4; show that outside the band does not always mean zero gradient.
SHOW THE MATH: negative-advantage piecewise surrogate; explain why taking the minimum changes the active side.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="A few epochs reuse one batch">

An epoch is one pass through a collected batch, and PPO makes several such passes while keeping its old probabilities and advantages fixed.

{/*
IDEA: minibatch ascent on the clipped policy surrogate plus a separately trained value loss; few epochs per rollout batch, then collect fresh data. Separate policy loss from critic loss and optional entropy bonus.
WIDGET: GridWorldLab mode="ppo-epochs" batch=64 minibatch=16 epochs=4 epsilon=0.2 lr=0.03 seed=31. Frozen rollout list, shuffled minibatch selection, ratio histogram and value-loss curve; favourable clipped samples marked separately from ratios merely outside the band.
MOTION over t: four epoch segments; shuffle and select minibatches within each; update policy and critic, recompute ratios, highlight active/plateau branches.
TRY: find a sample outside the band whose surrogate gradient remains active.
NUMBERS: surrogate, value loss, out-of-band ratio fraction and actual plateau fraction per epoch; keep these last two readouts distinct. Freeze GAE during epochs.
SHOW THE CODE: collect batch, freeze old log-probs/advantages, shuffle minibatches, update for four epochs.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Clipping encourages small moves but does not enforce a wall">

Clipping removes the incentive for some large probability changes, but shared parameters can still move those probabilities, so the policy's actual drift must be measured.

{/*
IDEA: PPO clipping is not a hard ratio or KL bound; a sample's flat surrogate does not freeze its probability. Measure KL(old || new) across states visited in the fixed batch; optional early stopping uses measured KL.
WIDGET: PPOLab mode="drift" epsilons="0.1,0.2,0.4" epochs=4 batch=64 lr=0.03 seed=31. Same rollout and initial logits for each setting; old/new action bars, ratio extrema and exact categorical KL averaged over batch states.
MOTION over t: advance each run one minibatch at a time; ratios can leave the shaded band, and the measured KL cursor traces the change.
TRY: find an out-of-band ratio and check whether other samples' updates moved it there.
NUMBERS: min/max ratio, average KL and surrogate per epoch for each epsilon; probe before claiming smaller epsilon gives smaller drift. If no excursion occurs, adjust the documented step size or batch before writing that claim.
SHOW THE MATH: KL(pi_old || pi_new); distinguish this within-batch diagnostic from RLHF's KL to a fixed SFT reference.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Collect again after updating">

After a few epochs PPO collects a fresh batch with its updated policy, repeats the advantage and clipping calculations, and can use the same loop to optimise a reward model in RLHF.

{/*
IDEA: full on-policy iteration; pi_old resets to the current policy at each new rollout, unlike DQN's persistent replay buffer. Bridge to /learn/alignment/rlhf/ where rewards score responses and a separate fixed-reference KL penalty shapes them.
WIDGET: GridWorldLab mode="ppo-train" batch=64 epochs=4 iterations=100 gamma=0.9 lambda=0.95 epsilon=0.2 seed=37. Grid policy arrows, rollout batch and greedy evaluation return/critic-error curves; no reuse of earlier iterations' batches.
MOTION over t: loop through collect, estimate advantages, four training epochs, evaluate and refresh old policy; later markers advance iterations without putting narration ahead of the displayed batch.
TRY: pause at a batch refresh and see the probability ratios return to 1.
NUMBERS: evaluation return at iterations 0, 20 and 100, critic error and KL(old || new) per batch; probe all claims and report variability where needed.
SHOW THE CODE: complete PPO outer loop using shared RL helpers. Next Day 12 begins at /learn/alignment/sft/; PPO is reused in /learn/alignment/rlhf/.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Common misconceptions">

Some common misunderstandings about PPO's objective.

{/*
NO WIDGET. Misconceptions:
1. "PPO clamps every probability ratio inside the band." It clips an objective term, not the probability itself.
2. "Every sample outside the band stops contributing." Only the favourable direction plateaus; the advantage sign selects that direction.
3. "The old policy is the original policy from before training." It is the policy that collected this batch and is refreshed next iteration.
4. "PPO replays arbitrary old experience like DQN." Its few epochs reuse the current rollout batch before fresh collection.
5. "Clipping and RLHF's reference KL penalty are the same." One compares with the collection policy; the other penalizes drift from a fixed reference.
*/}

</Step>
