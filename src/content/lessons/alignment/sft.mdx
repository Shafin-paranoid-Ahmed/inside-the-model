A pretrained model completes text; SFT teaches it to answer.

<Step title="A base model continues text">

A model trained only to predict the next token treats a question as the start of a document, so it often continues the document, for example with more questions, instead of answering.

<Step title="Instruction-response datasets">

The fix is a dataset of instructions paired with the responses we want, written by people, and SFT simply keeps training the model on those pairs.

{/*
IDEA: demonstration data; what is in it (format, correctness, style); quality and diversity matter more than size (LIMA, VERIFY claim wording).
WIDGET: SFTLab mode="data" demos=24 demoAccuracy=0.8 seed=2. A scrolling list of demonstration pairs (prompt, chosen candidate) generated by simulated demonstrators; a tally panel of the demonstrations' features: answers, correct, average length, flattery.
MOTION over t: demonstrations slide in one by one; tally bars count up.
TRY: find a demonstration with a wrong answer; demonstrators make mistakes too.
NUMBERS: share correct, mean length of the 24 demos.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Training on demonstrations">

SFT is ordinary next-token training: it raises the log-probability of each demonstrated response, and the whole policy shifts toward answering.

{/*
IDEA: loss = -mean log pi(y_demo|x); gradient for the log-linear policy = phi(y_demo) - E_pi[phi]; theta moves from theta_base toward theta_SFT.
WIDGET: SFTLab mode="train" demos=24 demoAccuracy=0.8 steps=150 lr=0.05 (Adam from src/lib/nn/optim.ts). Left: theta as 6 labelled feature bars ("likes answering", "likes correct", "likes length", ...) signed colours. Right: probability bars for one prompt, and the NLL loss curve below.
MOTION over t: per training step the feature bars and the probability bars tween; loss curve draws; the prompt shown rotates every 50 steps.
TRY: watch which feature bar moves first.
NUMBERS: NLL at step 0 and 150; probability of "continues the list" before and after (averaged over prompts).
SHOW THE MATH: gradient of -log softmax for a log-linear model. SHOW THE CODE: PyTorch-style SFT step (cross-entropy on response tokens).
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Loss on the response tokens only">

In practice each training example is a single text containing both the prompt and the response, and the loss is usually counted only on the response tokens, so the model learns to answer rather than to write prompts.

Bottom: two runs, all tokens vs response only.

TRY: compare held-out response loss for the two runs.

*/}

</Step>

<Step title="It learns a behaviour, not a list of answers">

Because the same parameters are shared across all prompts, SFT changes how the model behaves on questions it never saw in the demonstrations.

{/*
IDEA: generalisation of format and style. Held-out prompts improve because theta moved along shared features.
WIDGET: SFTLab mode="heldout". Base vs SFT probability bars side by side for the 2 held-out prompts.
MOTION over t: bars tween from base to SFT as the timeline passes through the training steps (same run as step 3, but only held-out prompts shown).
TRY: compare the probability of a correct answer on a held-out prompt before and after.
NUMBERS: that probability before and after for both held-out prompts.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Imitation caps quality">

SFT copies the demonstrations, mistakes and habits included, so on its own it cannot become more accurate than the people it imitates.

{/*
IDEA: maximum likelihood in this family matches feature averages: at the optimum E_pi[phi] = E_demo[phi] (moment matching). So SFT correctness tracks demonstrator correctness. Motivates preference learning in /learn/alignment/rlhf/.
WIDGET: SFTLab mode="cap" demoAccuracies="0.6,0.8,0.95" steps=300. For each demonstrator accuracy: a line of SFT expected correctness over training, with a horizontal dashed line at the demonstrators' rate (--text).
MOTION over t: three curves draw; each converges onto its dashed line.
TRY: notice that no curve ends above its line.
NUMBERS: final SFT correctness per demonstrator accuracy (should match to about 2 decimals; quote sim values).
SHOW THE MATH: setting the gradient to zero gives E_pi[phi] = E_demo[phi].
In real models: SFT can sometimes beat noisy demonstrators by averaging, but systematic habits are copied (Gudibande et al. 2023 on imitation).
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="Common misconceptions">

What SFT does and does not do.

{/*
NO WIDGET. Misconceptions:
1. "SFT teaches the model new knowledge." Mostly it teaches format and behaviour; knowledge comes from pretraining (LIMA's superficial alignment hypothesis, cite carefully).
2. "More demonstrations always help." Low-quality demonstrations are copied faithfully.
3. "The loss includes the prompt." Usually masked.
4. "SFT and pretraining use different losses." Same cross-entropy, different data.
*/}

</Step>
