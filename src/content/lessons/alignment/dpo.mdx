It turns out the reward model and the RL loop can be folded into one simple loss.

<Step title="The policy RLHF is aiming for">

The RLHF objective, maximise reward while paying a KL penalty, has an exact best answer: the reference policy reweighted by the exponential of the reward divided by the penalty strength.

{/*
IDEA: pi*(y|x) = pi_ref(y|x) exp(r(x,y)/beta) / Z(x). In the day's log-linear policy this is exactly theta* = theta_ref + w_RM/beta (src/lib/align/optimal.ts).
WIDGET: PreferenceLab mode="optimal" beta slider (0.02 to 1, log scale), default 0.1, rewardModel from the rlhf lesson's run. Bars: pi_ref (ghost), pi* (solid) for one prompt; the PPO beta=0.1 end state as tick marks.
MOTION over t: beta sweeps from large to small; pi* bars move from pi_ref toward the RM's favourite; at beta = 0.1 the PPO ticks line up with the bars.
TRY: drag beta to 0.02 and see which candidate takes over.
NUMBERS: max difference between PPO's final probabilities and pi* at beta 0.1 (should be small; quote the sim); Z(x) for the shown prompt.
SHOW THE MATH: derivation of pi* via the Gibbs inequality, 5 lines.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="The DPO loss on one pair">

Solving that formula for the reward and putting it into the Bradley-Terry model gives a loss that uses only the policy's own log-probabilities on the chosen and rejected responses.

{/*
IDEA: L = -log sigma(beta [ (log pi(y_w) - log pi_ref(y_w)) - (log pi(y_l) - log pi_ref(y_l)) ]); Z cancels. Gradient weight sigma(-margin): pairs the model already gets right count less.
WIDGET: PreferenceLab mode="dpo-pair" pair=0 beta=0.1 steps=40. One preference pair card; four log-prob readouts; margin on a number line; sigma curve with the current point; gradient arrows on chosen (up) and rejected (down) bars.
MOTION over t: repeated gradient steps on this single pair; the point climbs the sigma curve; the gradient weight shrinks.
TRY: watch the gradient weight fall as the margin grows.
NUMBERS: loss and margin at step 0 and 40; gradient weight at both.
SHOW THE MATH: from pi* to the DPO loss in 4 lines. SHOW THE CODE: DPO loss in PyTorch (about 8 lines).
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="The implicit reward">

The trained policy carries its own reward model: beta times the log-ratio of its probability to the reference probability scores every response.

{/*
IDEA: r_hat(y) = beta (log pi(y) - log pi_ref(y)); here equal to beta (theta - theta_ref) . phi up to a per-prompt constant; compare with the separately trained RM.
WIDGET: PreferenceLab mode="implicit" beta=0.1. Scatter of implicit reward vs RM score over all candidates; and the two weight-vector bar charts side by side.
MOTION over t: points move from the origin to their final positions as the DPO run progresses; the correlation readout updates.
TRY: find which feature weight differs most between the two.
NUMBERS: correlation between implicit reward and RM score at the end; the largest weight difference.
*/}

</Step>

<Step title="DPO against PPO">

DPO is simpler and stable, but it can only learn from the pairs it was given, while PPO samples new responses and gets them scored as it goes.

{/*
IDEA: trade-offs: offline vs online data; no RM to inspect; can drive both chosen and rejected likelihoods down and shift mass to responses absent from the data; strongly overfits deterministic preferences as beta shrinks (IPO motivation).
WIDGET: PreferenceLab mode="compare" betas="0.1,0.02" steps=300. Panels: (a) gold vs KL paths for PPO and DPO; (b) chosen and rejected log-prob over DPO training at beta 0.02.
MOTION over t: paths trace together; log-prob curves draw.
TRY: check whether the chosen responses' probability actually rises during DPO.
NUMBERS: final gold and KL for each method and beta; change in mean chosen log-prob. PROBE FIRST: only claim "chosen likelihood falls" if the sim shows it.

</Step>

<Step title="Common misconceptions">

Common misreadings of DPO.

{/*
NO WIDGET. Misconceptions:
1. "DPO has no reward model." It has an implicit one, defined by the policy itself.
2. "DPO is the same as SFT on chosen answers." It also pushes rejected answers down, weighted by how wrong the model is.
3. "DPO always raises the chosen response's probability." Only the margin is guaranteed to grow.
4. "DPO and PPO give the same model." Same optimum in theory, different data and optimisation in practice.
*/}

</Step>
