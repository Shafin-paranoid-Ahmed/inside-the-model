# Day 10 plan: Large language models

Six lessons, one model family. Step-by-step briefs with exact props live as MDX comments in `src/content/lessons/llms/*.mdx`. Read the brief for your lesson before building.

**Model and data decision.** Every distribution, attention map and loss on Day 10 comes from real tiny causal transformers built on Day 9's `src/lib/transformer/` core. They are trained once, offline, by one committed deterministic script, and their weights ship as small int8 binary assets that are fetched when the widget becomes visible. The weights never go into the JS bundle. Live training in the browser is not viable here: a 30k-parameter character LM needs thousands of steps at about 0.2 s each in JS, which is minutes per page. Inference is cheap (a 64-character forward pass takes a few ms, and decode with a KV cache is far less), so it runs live on the main thread and no Day 10 widget needs a worker. Tokens are characters: the vocabulary is about 35 and fits a bar chart, and the lessons say once that real LLMs use BPE (link `transformers/tokenization`).

## 3. Reuse and what Day 10 needs from Day 9's `src/lib/transformer/`

Reuse: `createRng`, `_shared` (FigureCard, Timeline, createLoop, readColors), the `exp` idiom from `Seq2SeqAttention/sim.ts`, and the softmax/cross-entropy wording from `foundations/softmax-cross-entropy`. Adam: either `src/lib/nn/optim.ts` (if it works on flat typed arrays) or a flat-array Adam inside the script.

**Required from the Day 9 core (precise list):**
1. A config `{ vocab, ctx, d, layers, heads, ff (0 = attention-only), posEmb: 'learned' | other, tied }` and **all parameters in one flat Float32/Float64 array with a named offset table** (as in seq2seq's `OFF`), so `load.ts` can fill it from int8 data.
2. `forward(params, cfg, tokens) → { logits (T×V), attn (per layer and head, T×T, causal) }`.
3. Incremental decode: `prefill(params, cfg, tokens) → { cache, logits }` and `decodeStep(params, cfg, cache, token) → logits`. The cache holds per-layer preallocated K and V buffers plus a length. A test checks that it equals `forward` within 1e-6.
4. Training: `lossAndGrad(params, cfg, batch) → { loss, grad }` with a hand-written backward pass, gradient-checked. The script needs nothing else.
5. Determinism: the arithmetic-only `exp`, no `Math.exp`/`Math.pow` inside the forward pass.

If Day 9 ships only items 1–2, Day 10 adds 3–4 **to the same module** (coordinate with the Day 9 owner; never fork a second transformer). If Day 9 trains its own tiny model, the house model must not replace it. They are separate files.

## 4. Build order

1. Day 9 core with items 1–5 → `src/lib/lm/` + `lm-core.test.ts` → `scripts/train-lm.ts` and the `house` job (this is the slow part, so run it in the background; expect tens of minutes) → commit the outputs to the repo (the owner commits).
2. In parallel, once `house.bin` exists: **SamplingLab** (smallest, proves the loader), **PretrainLab**, **KVCacheLab**.
3. `scaling` job (the longest, about 40 runs, possibly over an hour single-threaded) → **ScalingLab**. Its `published` and `overtrain` modes need no sweep and can be built first.
4. `icl` + `icl-sweep` jobs → **ICLLab**. `facts` (+ optional `facts-idk`) → **HallucinationLab**. These two are independent of each other.
Every lesson agent probes its numbers from the shipped files (BUILDING.md §10 stage 3) before writing prose.

## 5. Risks and open questions for the owner

- **Offline training, browser inference.** The weights are real and reproducible from a committed script, but they are not trained on the page (unlike Seq2SeqAttention). Is that acceptable? If you want something visibly trained live, the cheapest place is the pretraining `learning` step: a smaller model trained live for about 300 steps in a worker, at the cost of about 1 minute of compute per visit.
- **Binary assets outside the 40 KB JS budget.** `house.bin` is ≤ 40 KB and the facts model ≤ 40 KB, both fetched lazily. They do not count toward the JS budget, but they are bytes on the page. Is that OK?
- **Corpus in the repo** (`scripts/data/alice.txt`, about 300 KB, public domain). Alternatively the script could download it from Gutenberg at a pinned URL, which is less reproducible.
- **Tiny-model results may not cooperate.** Our scaling exponents and tokens-per-parameter ratio will differ from Chinchilla's. The ICL phase change or a clean induction head may not form, and the "unknown" variant may not generalise. Rule: the prose reports what the probe shows and labels published numbers as published. If a demo fails outright, change the training setup in the script, never the prose alone.
- The scaling sweep caps D at one epoch of about 270 K training characters, so the data axis spans only about 1.5 decades. A larger public-domain corpus would widen it. Owner's choice.
- Every published constant (Chinchilla fit, GPT-3/Chinchilla N and D, Llama configs, accelerator spec, Llama 3 token count) is marked VERIFY in the briefs. The lesson agent must check each one against the primary source before quoting it.
- The pretraining `data` mode uses about 12 sample documents written for the figure. They are labelled as such and are the only non-real data on Day 10.

### House model (pretraining, sampling, KV cache)

Config: 2 layers, 2 heads, d 36, ReLU MLP 144, context 64, learned positions, pre-LN, tied; vocab 38. Corpus 303,013 characters; 50 contiguous chunks, every 10th held out (272,700 train / 30,313 validation characters; 4,230 training and 470 validation non-overlapping 64-character windows). 6,000 Adam updates, batch 16 random 64-character windows (1,024 tokens per step), lr 0.003, 200-step warmup, cosine to 10%. N = 35,822 (32,112 non-embedding), D = 6,144,000 tokens (22.5 epochs), C = 6ND = 1.321e12 FLOP; GPT-3's 6 × 175e9 × 300e9 is 2.39e11 times larger.

- **Acceptance (passed):** validation loss after int8 quantisation **1.5044** nats/char (perplexity 4.50; float weights 1.5037), against unigram 3.0031 and bigram 2.3771 (add-0.5 cross-entropies on validation; plain training-text entropies 3.0063 and 2.3448) and uniform ln 38 = 3.6376. ### Scaling sweep

House architecture with ff = 4d, widths d = 8, 12, 16, 24, 36, 48, 64 (N = 2,614 … 106,662 total; 1,760 … 100,096 non-embedding), budgets D = 8,448 … 270,592 tokens (×2 steps, the largest = one epoch of shuffled non-overlapping training windows, smaller budgets are prefixes), batch 4 × 64 tokens, each run its own warmup + cosine. Each (width, budget) was trained at lr 0.003, 0.006 and 0.01 and **the best of the three is kept** (field `lr`); with a single lr the large models were badly mis-tuned (lr 0.01 made d = 64 worse than d = 24 at one epoch). C = 6 × N(total) × D spans 1.33e8 … 1.73e11 FLOP. 42 runs, each with ~14 curve points (validation loss on the first 32 validation windows) and `validLoss` on all validation windows.

- Final validation loss, smallest → largest D: d = 8: 3.035, 2.992, 2.868, 2.681, 2.568, 2.487; d = 64: 2.791, 2.672, 2.550, 2.483, 2.410, 2.299. Best run: d = 36 at one epoch, 2.1385. Every one-epoch run is still near or above the bigram baseline (2.377) except d ≥ 36: this is a data-starved regime (the house model needed 22 epochs to reach 1.50).
- Compute frontier (15 Pareto points): fit L = E + a C^(−b) gives **b = 0.0886, E = 0.885, a = 11.66, R² = 0.962**; a pure power law (E = 0) gives b = 0.0583, R² = 0.961. Kaplan's published compute exponent is about 0.050 (VERIFY); ours is a character model on 0.3 MB of text.
- **IsoFLOP: no interior optimum.** At every compute budget the smallest width whose runs reach that budget wins, because the one-epoch cap stops smaller models from using more data. Tokens per parameter at those edge optima: 10.7, 23.7, 52.6, 31.3, 26.0, 13.6, 6.8 for C = 4.4e8 … 5.2e10, so **no N_opt exponent or tokens-per-parameter ratio can be fitted** (`nOptExponent` is absent). The `isoflop` mode must either show these edge minima honestly or rely on the published Chinchilla fit for the U shape. A larger corpus would fix it (owner's choice, §5).

### In-context learning (`icl`, `icl-log`, `icl-sweep`)

**Changed from the brief:** the training task is **single-letter pairs only** (`c>f g>b ...`, 12 pairs per 50-token sequence, shift 0–7 per sequence, loss on answer letters only), and the model uses **RoPE** positions. Learned absolute positions stayed at chance (2.08 nats = ln 8) for 3,000–8,000 steps in four configurations; mixed 1–4-letter words stalled or learned single letters only (4-letter per-letter accuracy 0.13–0.33). The brief's "4-letter queries" are therefore measured as **a 4-letter word asked one letter per pair**: 4 single-letter examples, then `q1>a1 q2>a2 q3>a3 q4>` with earlier answers written in; exact match = all four letters right. Formats: unseen symbols such as `=` map to `<unk>` (id 11).

Config: 2 layers, 2 heads, d 24, MLP 24, context 50, RoPE, pre-LN, tied; vocab `a`–`h`, `>`, space, newline, `<unk>`. 4,000 updates, batch 32, lr 0.003, seed 11.

- **Acceptance (passed, after int8):** accuracy over 500 seeded tasks at k = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 examples: **0.124, 1.000, 0.994, 1.000, 1.000, 1.000** (chance 0.125). Query letters absent from the examples (novel): 1.000, 0.998, 1.000, 1.000 at k = 1, 2, 4, 6, so it infers the shift rather than copying. Random-answer examples (k = 4): 0.140. One wrong example (the last of 4): 0.232. Formats at k = 4: trained 1.000, `=` separator 0.302, newline between pairs 0.262. 4-letter word: per-letter 1.000, exact 1.000.
- **Shots probe** (shift 3, query `g`, answer `b`, examples `c>f a>d e>h h>c b>e d>g` none containing g): p(b) = 0.102 with 0 examples (top guess "c"), 0.9985 with 1, 0.9987 with 3.
- **Attention: no clean induction head.** The textbook induction weight (query `>` onto the answer of the earlier example with the same source letter) stays at 0.01–0.15 for every head through training. Averaged over 200 four-example prompts, from the query `>`: layer 1 heads look at earlier `>` and space positions (head 1: arrows 0.47, spaces 0.46; head 2: spaces 0.44, arrows 0.20, answers 0.15); layer 2 heads look at the query letter (0.54, 0.62) and the example answers (0.41, 0.28). On the default prompt `c>f g>b a>d e>h g>` layer 2 head 1 puts 0.993 on the query letter `g`; layer 2 head 2 puts 0.882 on `g`, 0.077 on the answer `d` and 0.037 on the answer `b`. p(b) = 0.998. `icl-log.shownHead` = layer 2, head 2 (zero-based layer 1, head 1), the layer-2 head whose pattern changed most.

What this shows, honestly: **the jump is not explained by the metric and is not monotone in size.** Each run either leaves the loss plateau (all metrics near perfect) or does not (all at chance, mean log-prob ≈ 4 ln 8 = 8.32), so per-letter accuracy and log-probability are as all-or-nothing as exact match, and exact ≈ per-letter⁴ holds only trivially (0.0002 vs 0, 1 vs 1; d = 48: 0.255⁴ = 0.0042 vs measured 0.006). Whether a run escapes depends on the run: at 4,000 updates only d = 24 and 32 had escaped; doubling to 8,000 added d = 12, while d = 8, 16 and 48 stayed on the plateau. The emergence step should present this as "abrupt per run, set by training dynamics, not cleanly by size", per the brief's own fallback ("metric choice explains some, not necessarily all"). A smoother size sweep would need several seeds per size or per-size learning rates.

### Facts model (hallucination)

200 invented people (seeded syllables, seed 1212), birth months drawn from a seeded non-uniform prior. Training occurrences: 20 (10 people), 10 (20), 5 (30), 2 (40), 1 (50), 0 (50), so 1/3 of the seen facts occur exactly once. Each epoch adds 30% context-copy lines (`x was born in m. x was born in m.`) using 3,000 *other* fresh names, so copying is learned without leaking the 200. Vocab: a–z, space, `.`, 12 month tokens, `<unknown>` (never in training). Same architecture as the house model; 25 epochs, batch 16 lines, lr 0.003. Prompt form: `<name> was born in ` → next token.

- **Accuracy by occurrences (passed):** 20: 1.000, 10: 1.000, 5: 1.000, 2: 0.725, 1: 0.340, 0: 0.060 (chance 1/12 = 0.083). Mean confidence (top month, renormalised over months): 0.932, 0.812, 0.631, 0.409, 0.334, 0.354. Overall 0.545.
- **Calibration:** ECE 0.073 over all 200 (10 bins); unseen people: mean confidence 0.354 vs accuracy 0.06. Reliability bins (confidence decile: n, accuracy, mean confidence): 0.1: 14, 0.143, 0.173; 0.2: 54, 0.259, 0.246; 0.3: 29, 0.414, 0.348; 0.4: 23, 0.478, 0.449; 0.5: 18, 0.722, 0.550; 0.6: 17, 0.882, 0.643; 0.7: 11, 0.909, 0.762; 0.8: 22, 0.909, 0.855; 0.9: 12, 1.000, 0.931.
- **Objective:** averaged month distribution of the 50 unseen people vs occurrence-weighted training month frequencies: total variation 0.168. p(`<unknown>`) on unseen people 5.0e-4 on average (it is never the top token).
- **Abstain (coverage, error among answered):** threshold 0: 1.00, 0.455; 0.3: 0.66, 0.296; 0.5: 0.40, 0.125; 0.7: 0.225, 0.067; 0.9: 0.06, 0.000.
- **Retrieval** (unseen people): no context 0.06; correct fact in context 1.00; wrong fact (true month + 5) in context 0.00 correct, **1.00 copy the wrong month**.
- **Self-consistency** (k = 10 samples at T = 1 over the renormalised months, seeds 5000 + index): mean agreement 0.654 for seen-and-correct people, 0.408 unseen, 0.393 wrong answers; "agreement ≥ 0.5 means correct" is right for 71.0% of people. k = 3: 0.695 / 0.500 / 0.484, rule right for 69.0%.
- **Hook examples:** count 20 `sizor lebo` (true april) → april 0.906; count 1 `fufu pipa` (true june) → october 0.374 (june 0.125); unseen `nulis masov` → may 0.335, march 0.229; unseen `zuza rumar` → july 0.388. Never-trained probe names (`probeNames` in `facts-data.json`) behave the same: `tadi rosav` → november 0.389.
- **facts-idk: not shipped.** Training 100 extra names × 3 lines answering `<unknown>` (40 epochs) made the model say unknown for 26% of brand-new names but also for 6.7% of known people (at 25 epochs: 42% new names vs 21% known). That is weak generalisation with collateral damage, below the bar for a toggle; the `abstain` step should drop the variant and say so. The job remains in the script (`facts-idk`) for anyone who wants to probe it.
